Connectors

Thirty-four line treatments on the same 200px span, so the comparison is fair.

Grouped by job, not by look. Weight and taper handle emphasis and direction. Gradients replace arrowheads. Dash patterns carry state — planned, inferred, standby. Depth lifts one path above the rest. Composition builds a line from several strokes. Geometry is the route itself. Glow specimens sit on a dark band because that is the only place glow is real.

Watch out: a diagram should use two line treatments, three at the absolute most. Every additional one is a legend entry and a thing to remember. Direction cues stack badly too — taper plus gradient plus arrowhead is one idea said three times. And any treatment that fades, thins or glows loses contrast somewhere along its length, so never place a label on the weak end.

Described in words

Six groups. Weight and taper: hairline, standard, heavy, tapered, spindle, cased. Gradient: two-tone, fade-out, ghost ends, multi-stop, gradient-plus-taper, halo. Dash: dashed, dotted, dash-dot, graduated, planned-over-actual, solid-plus-dashed pair. Depth: cast shadow, soft halo, three-layer neon, gradient neon, embossed, trail. Composition: bundle, track, ribbon, pipe, beaded, segmented. Geometry: orthogonal, rounded elbow, bezier, arc, stepped, line-jump.

Each is captioned with the job it does, so the sheet is a selection guide rather than a swatch book.
