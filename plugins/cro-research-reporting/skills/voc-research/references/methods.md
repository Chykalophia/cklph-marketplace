# VoC Methods and Question Bank

## On-page polls (Hotjar, Microsoft Clarity surveys, Klaviyo forms, etc.)
- One open question, on the page with the leak, shown after engagement (scroll depth or time) or on exit intent.
- Good questions:
  - PDP: "Is there anything stopping you from buying this today?" / "What else would you need to know to decide?"
  - Cart/checkout: "Was anything confusing or missing at this step?"
  - Pricing/landing: "What's the one thing holding you back from signing up?"
- Run until ~200 responses or 2 weeks; low response rates (1–3%) are normal.

## Post-purchase survey (thank-you page or email within a day)
1. What were you looking for when you found us? (job to be done)
2. What nearly stopped you from buying? (the most useful question)
3. Which other options did you consider? (competitive alternatives)
4. What convinced you to buy from us? (persuasive elements to amplify)
5. How would you describe this product to a friend? (language bank)
6. Optional: one closed question for segmentation (first purchase? gift?)
Ask buyers about friction and non-buyers (via poll) about objections; the two lists differ.

## Review, ticket and return mining
- Sources: own product reviews (all star levels; 3-star reviews are the richest), competitor reviews on the same product type, Amazon reviews in the category, support tickets, chat transcripts, return reasons, sales-call notes.
- Pull at least 200 items per source when available; record source, date, rating, product.
- Code for: objections, anxieties, desired outcomes, use cases, comparisons, and exact phrases.

## User testing
- 5 participants per round per distinct segment; 2–3 rounds with fixes in between.
- Tasks match the funnel: "Find a pair of shoes for a wedding under $200 and get to the payment step."
- Record time on task, success/fail, and every point of hesitation. Severity = how many participants hit it × whether it blocked them.
- Remote unmoderated tools are fine for usability; use moderated sessions for motivation and pricing questions.
- Test on the device most of the traffic uses (usually mobile).

## Session recordings and heatmaps
- Use to confirm where a theme happens, not to find themes. Watch 20–30 sessions filtered to the leaking step (e.g. reached cart, did not check out).
- AI can summarise recordings in bulk; a human should watch at least 10 before trusting the summary.

## Sample size rules of thumb
- Proportions in a representative random sample: 200 responses gives roughly ±7 points at 95% confidence on a 50% share; 100 gives ±10. Opt-in polls and post-purchase surveys are not random samples, so treat their shares as descriptive of respondents, not as population estimates.
- A theme mentioned by fewer than ~5% of a 200-response survey is a lead, not a finding, unless it also shows up in another source.
